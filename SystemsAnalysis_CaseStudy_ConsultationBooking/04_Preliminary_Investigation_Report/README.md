## 1. Introduction

This report summarizes the preliminary investigation of a request for an Instructor Consultation Booking System. Its purpose is to help the program head decide whether the project should move to the systems analysis phase.

The investigation followed the textbook's preliminary investigation steps (Chapter 2): understand the problem, define the scope and constraints, perform fact-finding, analyze project usability, cost, benefits and schedule, evaluate feasibility, and present the results.

## 2. Systems request summary

Walter Dvorak, Instructor BIS, main campus, requested a new system in October 2026. Students and project groups book consultations by email and a paper door sheet, which causes 2–4 rounds of email per booking, double-bookings, missed meetings and no meeting records. He asked for a simple system where students can see his open slots, book and cancel themselves, receive reminders, and where he can keep private notes. The full system request form is in Deliverable 01.

## 3. Findings

The problem is real, measurable and solvable with tools the college already owns.

**Fact-finding:** a 30-minute interview with Walter and a questionnaire of 30 students (Deliverable 03); a CATWOE analysis, fishbone diagram and as-is process model (Deliverable 01).

**Key findings**

1.  **Time lost:** 40–60 booking emails a week at peak times, about 3–4 instructor hours a week.

2.  **Errors:** 2–3 double-bookings a month, because the paper sheet and Outlook are not linked.

3.  **Missed meetings:** about 15% of booked meetings are missed; 60% of students have missed one.

4.  **No record:** notes are kept in a personal Excel file, often skipped.

5.  **Privacy risk:** student names are posted on a public door sheet.

6.  **User demand:** 80% of students find email booking slow; 70% would rather self-book online.

**Scope:** a booking system for one instructor (Walter), with self-booking, reminders, a dashboard, private notes and basic reports. Use by other instructors is out of scope for this release.

**Constraints:** little or no budget; must use Microsoft 365 and college logins; must meet FOIP privacy rules; ready before the Winter 2027 term.

**Feasibility (Deliverable 02):** Option B (Microsoft Bookings) is the only option that is operationally, technically, economically, schedule and legally feasible.

## 4. Recommendations

**Proceed with the project, using Option B: configure Microsoft Bookings.**

1.  Approve the project to move to the systems analysis and design phase.

2.  Ask IT Services to enable Microsoft Bookings for Walter's account, with Outlook and Teams integration.

3.  Build the GUI prototype (Deliverable 08) from the final requirements (Deliverable 05) and test it with Walter and a small group of students.

4.  Retire the paper door sheet once the system is live.

5.  Review use after the first 6 weeks of Winter 2027 before offering it to other instructors.

Options not recommended: doing nothing (problems continue), a custom app (costs more than it saves over 3 years and will not be ready in time) and a commercial service (student data stored outside college systems).

## 7. Expected benefits

The system saves about \$5,250 a year in instructor time and returns 182% over 3 years.

**Tangible benefits**

- About 105 instructor hours a year freed for teaching and advising (3.5 hours a week × 30 weeks), worth about \$5,250 at \$50 an hour

- Payback in about 8 months; 3-year net benefit of about \$10,170

- Double-bookings reduced from 2–3 a month to none

- Missed meetings reduced from about 15% to a target of under 5%

**Intangible benefits**

- Faster, easier booking for students, on any device

- A searchable record of every consultation and project-group meeting

- Better privacy: no student names on a public door sheet

- A model other instructors could adopt later
